# Detailed Method: Fruit‑Peel Cold Brew Fertilizer

This document explains the full, step‑by‑step process for making a clean, low‑odor, cold‑fermented fruit‑peel infusion for plants.  
The goal is to create a gentle, microbially active liquid using only fruit peels, water, and time — without rot, slime, or bad smells.

---

## 1. Collecting Fruit Peels

Suitable peels:
- banana (recommended base)
- apple
- peach
- mango
- small amounts of citrus (optional, use lightly)

Avoid:
- moldy fruit
- peels with oils, butter, or seasoning
- frozen‑then‑thawed peels (they ferment unpredictably)

---

## 2. Baking the Peels

Baking:
- dries the peels
- prevents rot
- concentrates sugars for fermentation

**Instructions:**
1. Spread peels on a tray.
2. Bake at **90–120°C (195–250°F)**.
3. Bake until fully dry and crisp.
4. Let them cool completely.

Dry peels store well and grind cleanly.

---

## 3. Grinding the Peels

Grinding increases surface area and speeds extraction.

**Instructions:**
1. Break dried peels into pieces.
2. Grind using a blender, grinder, or mortar.
3. Aim for a **coarse powder** — not too fine.

This powder becomes your “tea.”

---

## 4. Filling the Tea Bag

Use:
- biodegradable tea bags  
- reusable cloth bags  
- muslin bags  

**Recommended ratio:**  
Use the equivalent of **1/3 of a dried banana peel (as powder) per 250 ml of water**.  
Scale up or down depending on jar size.

**Instructions:**
1. Add the appropriate amount of peel powder to the tea bag.
2. Fill the bag about halfway so the powder has room to expand.
    - (Overfilling reduces water flow and slows fermentation.)
3. Tie or seal securely.

---

## 5. Preparing the Jar

Use a clean glass jar (500 ml to 1 L).

**Instructions:**
1. Fill the jar with cold water.
2. Add the tea bag using the ratio:  
   **1/3 dried banana peel per 250 ml water.**
3. Cover the jar with **breathable material**:
   - mesh
   - cloth
   - a tea bag
   - paper towel  
4. Secure with a rubber band.

**Do not seal the jar.**  
Fermentation needs airflow.

---

## 6. Cold Fermentation

This method produces a **clear, tea‑colored infusion**, not a cloudy compost tea.

**Instructions:**
1. Leave the jar at room temperature.
2. Keep it out of direct sunlight.
3. Steep for **1–2 weeks**.

## Temperature Notes

This method has been tested in warm weather (summer conditions).  
Fermentation is most active when the environment is warm, and the process may slow down in cooler seasons.

General guidance:
- Works best in **warm room temperatures**. (Ideally it works between 21-26 Celsius)
- Fermentation may take longer in cooler weather.
- If the room is too cold, bubbles may appear more slowly.

The method still relies on:
- cold water
- airflow
- indirect light

Only the **ambient temperature** affects the speed, not the water temperature.

---

### Aroma progression

- **First few days:**  
  A pleasant fruit‑vinegar smell appears. It’s clean, light, and slightly tangy — a sign of healthy early fermentation.

- **As it continues:**  
  The vinegar note gradually softens. Timing varies depending on temperature, peel blend, and grind size. The smell becomes milder over time.

- **Later stage:**  
  The final aroma is gentle and clean, similar to a lightly fermented fruit tea. It never becomes harsh or rotten if the process is healthy.

---

### Visual signs of healthy fermentation

- clear bubbles forming around the tea bag  
- liquid stays clear, like black tea  
- color deepens gradually  
- no cloudiness  
- no surface film or mold  

---

### Signs something is wrong

- cloudy liquid  
- rotten or sulfur smell  
- fuzzy mold on the surface  
- no bubbles at all after 10–14 days  

---

## 7. Using the Liquid

You can:
- dilute 1:8 for most plants 
- dilute more for sensitive plants
- use weekly or bi‑weekly  

Use within a few days after removing the tea bag.

---

## 8. Zero‑Waste Disposal

The used tea bag and contents are:
- biodegradable  
- compostable  
- safe to bury in soil  

Just compost it or bury it in a plant pot.

---

## 9. Notes & Tips

- Banana peel should be **50% or more** of any blend.
- Cold water prevents anaerobic rot.
- A breathable cover is essential.
- Grinding improves consistency but is optional.
- Baking is what keeps this method clean and low‑odor.

---
